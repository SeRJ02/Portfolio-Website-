Automation

EarnKaro — Broadcast Automation Tool

3,000 users. 10 account managers. Personal WhatsApp numbers. This was not a system.

Situation

EarnKaro had over 3,000 users being communicated to through individual account managers — each using their personal office WhatsApp number. 10 people doing the same job, differently, every day. More than 5,000 support chats a month were coming in — commission changes users didn't know about, validation delays nobody told them about, and sale windows they missed. And buried inside all of this was a structural flaw nobody had documented — if a user hadn't saved the account manager's number, the broadcast message simply never reached them. It just didn't send. Talked to 100 users. More than 50% said the same thing: on-time communication for commission changes and sale alerts.

Task

Move all communication off individual WhatsApp numbers and onto a central broadcast system through CleverTap and WhatsApp automation — reliably, at scale, for every user regardless of whether they'd saved a number.

Action

Started with the data. 5,000 CRM chats a month, categorised by type, pointed directly at three recurring communication failures: commission changes, validation delays, and pre-sale messages. Built the full plan of execution from scratch — process design, logic architecture, and a phased roadmap broken into three trigger types. Managed the data team through writing the automation codes. Wrote the engineering specs for handoff and stayed close through the build. Three months from problem identified to fully shipped. Rebuilt for Telegram within the same sprint.

Result

  • Rolled out to 3,900 users
  • CRM support chats dropped from 30% to 10% — and continued falling
  • 3,000 users on Telegram communication bot in under 5 days
  • Manual broadcast effort across the team down 40%
  • Rookie Rockstar Award Q1

What I'd Do Differently

Shipped as three separate projects — commission changes, validation alerts, and sale messages. The logic was interconnected enough that building it as one unified system from the start would have saved a sprint and a half. I treated them as separate problems because they came from separate complaints. They were the same problem wearing three different hats.
